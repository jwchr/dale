## What the rider sees

1. **One small line on the home screen**, under the next buses, only when the rider's trip crosses the Hudson: "Lincoln Tunnel to NY: 32 min, 5 more than usual · Port Authority, 1 min ago" / "Túnel Lincoln a NY: 32 min, 5 más de lo normal · Port Authority, hace 1 min". Normal traffic (extra time under 3 minutes) reads "normal" / "normal"; a closure or missing data says so ("Lincoln Tunnel data unavailable" / "Sin datos del Túnel Lincoln"), never a guess. The direction is the trip's own (see "Which crossing a trip uses"), so an evening ride home shows the NJ-bound time. A trip over the George Washington Bridge shows the range across the upper and lower levels for its direction ("GWB to NY: 18 to 25 min"), since Dale cannot tell which level the bus takes. Tapping it opens the crossing screen.
2. **Crossing screen** (one screen, three short sections, each with source and age):
   - **Crossings now:** Lincoln Tunnel and George Washington Bridge (upper and lower levels), both directions, live minutes against the usual minutes for this time, closed flags.
   - **Posted delays:** the Port Authority's own messages for these crossings, including XBL bus lane openings and closures.
   - **Along your route:** incidents and construction near the roads this trip's bus actually drives, nearest first (by distance along the route ahead of the locked bus, or of the rider's chosen boarding stop when no bus is locked), at most 5 with "Show all". 511NJ covers New Jersey and the crossings, not Manhattan streets, so an empty list says "No active NJ or crossing incidents reported · 511NJ" / "Sin incidentes activos en NJ o cruces · 511NJ", never that the whole route is clear.
   A short note says crossing times measure car traffic; buses in the XBL bus lane (weekday mornings to New York) can skip part of that queue.
3. **No changed arrival times at first** (see "A new estimate" below). Settings gain nothing.

Before building the screens, a mock-up of the line and the crossing screen, in English and Spanish, goes to Jeff for his OK (Nov 24 design check). Every label, status, disclaimer and header is in both languages.

## How it works

- **Worker, read-through only.** A new `GET /v1/crossings` on the existing Worker returns one small normalized answer, the same for every rider. It takes no location, route, stop, trip or vehicle parameters; the phone picks its crossing, direction and nearby incidents itself, so no rider position or bus identity ever reaches the Worker or its logs. No extra NJ Transit calls. The Port Authority crossing times come from the background once-a-minute reading (see "A new estimate"), so that agency gets one request a minute in total, and the Worker keeps the last good reading with its time to show its age when a fetch fails. 511NJ and the Port Authority alerts feed are read only on request, each kept in Cloudflare's edge cache for 60 seconds; that cache is per data center, so the load is about one request a minute per data center serving riders (a handful for this region), plus a 30-minute stale copy of each used only to show "last good" with its age when that feed fails; a cold data center with no copy says unavailable without an age. The phone refreshes the answer at most once a minute while the screen or line is visible and stops when the app is in the background, which keeps Worker requests far under the free 100,000 a day. PR 2 picks one 511NJ feed and measures its parsing against the free plan's 10 ms CPU limit, using a lighter feed or pre-filtering if it does not fit. The browser never calls the agencies directly (their cross-site rules are not checked, and the cached Worker read keeps the load on them to the figures above).
- **Normalized answer.** Per crossing and direction: live minutes, usual minutes, extra minutes (live minus usual, never below zero for display), closed, data available, observed time, fetched time, source name. Per incident: type, road, short description, point, start time, source, fetched time. Only these listed fields are copied out; everything else, including staff names, colors and other display fields, is dropped, and alert text is checked for staff names before it is shown. The Worker never logs a raw agency response, even on a parse error. Text is shown as the agency wrote it, labeled with its source, and not translated by a machine; on the Spanish screen that label is in Spanish: "(en inglés, de 511NJ)" or "(en inglés, de Port Authority)", for both 511NJ items and Port Authority posted delays. Each source also carries its own last-update time (511NJ's feed date, the alert's own time, the crossing reading's clock time), not only Dale's fetch time, so a frozen feed shows as old. Reading the crossing rows, in this order: closed flag set means "Closed"; otherwise data not available, or a missing, zero or negative live time, means "unavailable" (never 0 min or "normal"); otherwise extra under 3 minutes is "normal".
- **Clock time without a date.** The Port Authority gives "5:15 PM" only, and the Worker's own clock runs in UTC, so every date here is the Eastern (America/New_York) date, never the UTC one. Dale tries that clock time on yesterday, today and tomorrow in Eastern time (both readings of the repeated 1 AM hour in November; a time that doesn't exist in the March gap is unavailable) and takes the latest one no more than 5 minutes after the fetch. A reading more than 10 minutes old is shown as stale with its age; more than 30 minutes, as unavailable. For 511NJ, age is the feed's own build time (its `lastBuildDate`, or `pubDate` when that is missing) measured against the Worker's fetch clock: more than 10 minutes is stale, more than 30 is unavailable. An item's own start time only says how long the incident has lasted and never makes the feed stale. The alerts feed's `start_date` is also an Eastern date; if it filters by the day an alert started, Dale asks for yesterday and today and drops duplicates, so an alert posted at 11:30 PM still shows after midnight. Both daylight-saving changes are tested.
- **Which crossing a trip uses** comes from the trip's own route shape, never from its route number (Product rules: nothing inferred from a route number). A pattern whose shape passes within about 300 meters of a crossing's New Jersey approach uses that crossing, and the order of the shape's points gives the direction (New Jersey approach first means to New York; New York side first means to New Jersey). The 163, 164, 165 and 165T show the Lincoln Tunnel line; a trip with no crossing shows no line.
- **"Along your route"** keeps 511NJ items within about 500 meters of the trip's remaining shape (or the whole shape when no bus is locked), in both states' parts of the route that 511NJ covers.
- **Failure.** Any source down, slow (5-second timeout), malformed or stale shows that section as unavailable with its last good age; the other sections still show. No made-up delay. A closed crossing says "Closed (Port Authority)".

## A new estimate (later, only if it proves better)

Jeff asked whether Dale can calculate a new arrival estimate. It can try, but only shows one if the data proves it helps:

1. **Collect first, starting now.** Jeff, Nov 24, 2026: "Yeah, I want to start logging that data too, port authority crossings." The Worker saves every Port Authority crossing-times reading in the background, once a minute (the feed's own refresh), whether or not anyone is looking, into the private history archive with the bus history (D60, D77). It keeps the original JSON as fetched (the crossing-times file holds no names) and adds the fetch time; each fetch is stored once, keyed by its 1-minute collection slot in UTC (like `2026-11-24T22:15Z`), so a retry or a second copy of the Worker never saves it twice, and failed and unchanged fetches get their slot the same way; when readings are later turned into observations for the backtest, those are deduplicated by crossing, direction and the resolved observation time in epoch milliseconds, and saved readings are never changed or deleted (the archive's existing rules); it does not save the alerts feed, whose records include staff names, unless a later step strips them first. Readings are batched with the archive's existing hourly writes, so it adds no write per reading (the exact added write count is measured when built; not checked yet), under the existing write, storage and free-limit guards, never paid overflow. A failed or unchanged reading is logged as such, never filled in. This is a risky step under `docs/HISTORY_COLLECTION.md` (Neon review, Jeff's go-ahead to deploy).
2. **Backtest** with [#102](https://github.com/jwchr/dale/issues/102): for buses still before the tunnel, compare NJ Transit's own prediction against "NJ Transit prediction plus today's extra tunnel minutes" (and smarter versions) using archived actual arrivals at Port Authority, by hour, weekday and XBL hours.
3. **Show it only if it wins**, clearly beating NJ Transit's prediction on held-out days. It then appears as a second, labeled time ("Dale estimate with tunnel traffic: 6:21 PM") next to NJ Transit's, never replacing it, with its method one tap away. If it doesn't win, Dale keeps showing the traffic as context only and says so.

The history also answers Jeff's original question in #251 (best times to travel by day and season) once enough weeks are saved; that report is a later step, and anything public from it shows totals and averages only, never the saved readings themselves.
