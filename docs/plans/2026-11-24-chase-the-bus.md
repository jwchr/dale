## Sources, car routing and catch-up math

- Reuse current GTFS calendar, ordered stop calls, pickup restrictions, route variant and version-matched geometry; only stops served by this exact trip and allowing boarding qualify. Repeated stops need stop-sequence anchors. Terminal drop-off-only stops remain follow destinations, never catch-up options.
- Reuse the existing coalesced official GPS/prediction snapshot, with original observation/generated times and receipt time separate. No extra NJ Transit poller. Fresh official exact-trip predictions lead; schedules remain explicitly scheduled. Shared onboard positions, once independently released under #105, remain labeled supplemental rider evidence, never official arrival facts or automatic ETA replacements.
- Phone GPS provides position, accuracy and time, not roads or traffic. Keep fixes in memory on this phone; reject future/out-of-order fixes and uncertain road matches. Permission denial still permits following the bus without car estimates.
- **Required routing dependency:** prepare a bounded downloadable OpenStreetMap car-routing graph and licensed browser routing engine, evaluated locally first. MapLibre/PMTiles display tiles and GTFS shapes are not such a graph. Route entirely on the phone, respecting one-way streets, turn/access restrictions and car-legal stop approaches; retain graph date, license/attribution and travel-time model version.
- Prove graph coverage, download size, phone memory/CPU and a free static-hosting budget before choosing that engine/data package. Road access, safe drop-off/walking approaches and traffic quality need verification; no live traffic source is established. No new paid service, key or external routing request follows from this plan. If local routing cannot meet these needs, report the blocker and propose a privacy-reviewed alternative to Jeff; sending coordinates to a routing vendor is not an automatic fallback.
- For a qualified phone fix taken at time `t`, routing gives `Dcar(s)` road miles and `Tcar(s)` modeled driving duration to stop `s`. All times are epoch milliseconds. Compute `carReady(s) = now + Tcar(s) + approach/parking/walking allowance + boarding buffer`, using the current clock, not the fix time, so an old fix never makes the car look earlier. A fix older than 30 seconds or with accuracy worse than 100 meters gives no recommendation, only the visibly aged car position; show the allowance and its source or rider-entered value. Do not invent an unknown allowance, traffic delay or statistical confidence interval.
- Compare with a fresh exact-stop official bus prediction `busETA(s)`: `margin(s) = busETA(s) - carReady(s)`. Positive margin is an estimated opportunity, never a guarantee. Every driving time carries the notice "No live traffic: driving times assume normal roads" / "Sin tráfico en vivo: los tiempos de manejo suponen calles normales". If a supported bus arrival range exists, compare to its earliest bound; without one explicitly say prediction uncertainty is unknown. Scheduled-only comparison stays provisional and cannot earn a confident last-chance claim.
- For the bus itself, route to its last qualified point and label "to last bus position," with timestamp and model age. This is not a rendezvous time with a moving bus. No legal car route to bus-only roads/terminal areas means unavailable; do not silently snap to another road or substitute straight-line miles.
- Passed/skipped stops, off-route or ambiguous bus progress, stale GPS/predictions, missing pickup rules, inaccessible approaches or absent routing coverage prevent a supported recommendation. Unknown time at one stop does not erase known alternatives; incomplete coverage prevents a claim that no further catch is possible. Recompute on new evidence without changing lock identity.
- Private #250 arrival evidence is a dependency, not public raw history permission. Its Nov 21 owner-only read door needs its own reviewed, secret-gated, read-only corridor route; no rider data. Owner access does not authorize publishing per-trip archive records or turning prior buses' delays into this bus's live ETA.

## Privacy and Tessie secret boundary

- Chase opt-in is separate from "share my bus position." Car fixes, accuracy, paths and chosen personal locations stay in phone memory, out of storage, URLs, analytics, crash reports, caches, Worker and history. Stop geolocation on exit, permission revocation or suspension; resume only in the active consented session and mark missing updates honestly. A locked browser cannot guarantee seconds-level updates.
- A navigation tap discloses the selected public stop destination to Tessie/Tesla or the chosen map app; explain the recipient before the tap. Send no phone origin, car trail or bus coordinates. Maps links specify destination only; the external app's own location permission is separate. No automatic navigation command on GPS refresh or stop reranking.
- Jeff alone creates/sets the Tessie token directly in Cloudflare dashboard, existing Dale Worker, Settings → Variables and Secrets, type **Secret**, proposed name `TESSIE_API_KEY`. The selected Tesla identifier is also a Secret (`secret_text`), `TESSIE_VEHICLE_ID`, never source, a plain variable or client output. The live deploy refuses any binding it does not list (`ownerSecrets` in `scripts/deploy-live-worker.mjs`), so step 5 adds `TESSIE_API_KEY`, `TESSIE_VEHICLE_ID` and `TESSIE_OWNER_CODE_HASH` to that list, as `secret_text` only, in a reviewed PR before Jeff sets any of them. No token in chat, git, Wrangler plaintext vars, frontend build, browser storage or test fixtures. A repository secret would require a reviewed server-side deploy binding; static Pages cannot use it safely.
- The Worker reads `env.TESSIE_API_KEY` only for an authenticated explicit navigation POST, resolves an allowlisted public stop ID to a destination (the allowlist holds only boarding stops with a verified car-legal approach; drop-off-only terminals and bus-only stops are excluded), and sends that destination to the configured car using a fixed Tessie host and bearer header. Never accept a caller-supplied VIN, URL or arbitrary command; refuse redirects. Token/vehicle identity never return to the phone. [Cloudflare secret bindings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/secrets/), [Tessie authentication](https://developer.tessie.com/reference/authentication).
- Verify Tessie's documented [Share command](https://developer.tessie.com/reference/share), current entitlement, limits, permissions and destination format before implementation. Request one attempt rather than its default retries; a successful command acknowledgment means "sent," not proof navigation appeared or the rider arrived. No purchases, account setup, token discovery or real vehicle commands in this docs task.
- Gate the route with a separate owner code, not the household code (which the household rider also holds): its SHA-256 lives in the Secret `TESSIE_OWNER_CODE_HASH` and is checked with the existing constant-time `codeMatches`. The code travels only in the HTTPS POST body, never a URL; the phone keeps it only in memory for the session. Five wrong codes in an hour lock the route for an hour. Requests must come from the Dale Pages origin and carry the operation ID and the phone's send time (`sentAt`, epoch ms); a request more than 5 minutes from the Worker's clock is rejected as expired, and each duplicate record is kept at least until its `sentAt` plus 5 minutes has passed on the Worker's clock (so more than 10 minutes when a phone clock runs up to 5 minutes fast), so an old operation ID cannot be replayed while it would still be accepted; at most 10 navigation sends per hour. The wrong-code count, the lockout and the hourly send count are kept durably in the Durable Object, never in Worker memory (another edge copy would not see them). Each of their writes goes through `reserveSpareWrites`; with no spare write budget the request is refused with zero provider calls. During an active lockout a request is refused at once with zero storage writes, so repeated wrong codes cannot use up the free write budget that history collection needs. Scope any short-lived navigation capability to that car/action, cap requests, reject expired/replayed requests and cross-site requests. CORS/origin alone is not authentication. Missing verified owner gating keeps Tessie disabled.
- Give each tap an operation ID with bounded durable duplicate suppression before dispatch, independent of the history journal's evidence. The duplicate record is written through `reserveSpareWrites` before dispatch; with no spare write budget the tap is refused (fail closed) and no provider call is made. The Tessie call runs outside the Durable Object's startup lock (`blockConcurrencyWhile`), never inside a feed poll, with a 10-second timeout, so a slow provider cannot delay bus collection. Crash/timeout after dispatch means "delivery unconfirmed" with no automatic resend; a new explicit tap is needed. Never claim exactly-once delivery across Tessie and the Worker. Avoid destination/location storage even in the deduplication record; count its writes against free limits.
- Missing secret/setup, rejected authorization, sleeping/unreachable car, provider refusal, rate limit or timeout shows a translated failure/unconfirmed state and destination-only Maps fallback. No key echo or raw provider error. No background car telemetry polling or automatic wake loop. Disable the integration without deleting existing history.
- Outbound provider URLs (which contain the vehicle identifier), request/response bodies, authorization headers, vehicle paths and destination choices must be excluded from Worker/provider-facing diagnostics and traces; validate logging configuration before release. Responses use `no-store`; health exposes totals only. Tessie/Tesla process a chosen destination under their own policies, not Dale's memory-only retention promise.

Retain existing persistent guards and free headroom; refuse new work at limits rather than upgrade or delete evidence. No collector, archive, cadence or migration change is needed for Chase.
- Preserve immutable archives and verified archive-before-prune; crashes/retries cannot lose or double count history. Owner-only history reads are bounded and read-only; public history remains totals only. No new public endpoint exposes rider locations or raw history.

Do not relabel an overnight trip as today's new trip at midnight or project old geometry onto a new feed. All labels, warnings, consent, speech, accessible names and failures need reviewed EN/ES and 12-hour clocks.
